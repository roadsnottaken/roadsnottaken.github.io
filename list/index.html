What we build next is up to us. Again.

The viaduct lands, a new crossing of Burrard Inlet, more SkyTrain, a shoreline facing rising seas and a recovering Stanley Park forest are all on the table. In 1967, neighbours stopped a freeway through the city's core. The same kind of choice is in front of us now.

01 · Getting around

Move people, not lanes.

In 1972 the city turned down a freeway crossing. Now the question is how far trains, boats and buses should reach, and who gets to ride them.

SkyTrain keeps growingFall 2027 is when the Broadway Subway is set to open

The Broadway Subway is a 5.7 km Millennium Line extension with six underground stations, and it's scheduled to open in fall 2027. The UBC extension is still in planning, with no full construction funding. The 16 km Surrey–Langley extension is expected in late 2029.

A new crossing of Burrard Inlet5 crossing options studied in 2020, all tunnels or bridges for transit

TransLink is studying rapid transit to the North Shore through its Burrard Inlet Rapid Transit program. The study could eventually include a crossing just for transit. This time the debate is about trains and buses, not lanes into downtown.

Last timeA freeway tunnel was planned near Brockton Point, and Vancouverites killed it. See the map →

Sources Future essay · BC: Burrard Inlet Rapid Transit Study (2020)

The case for more local ferries6.05M SeaBus boardings in 2025, up 3.8%

The SeaBus runs every 10 to 15 minutes by day. In False Creek, private operators run small ferries. A 2022 Metro Vancouver study recommended a business case for a ferry linking Port Moody, Belcarra and Deep Cove, but the 2020 Burrard Inlet Rapid Transit study looked only at tunnels and bridges.

Last timeThe 1960s plans answered the inlet with a freeway crossing. By the end of 1972 it was dead, and five years later the SeaBus began sailing. See the map →

Sources TransLink SeaBus schedule · TransLink 2025 performance review · Metro Vancouver parks access study (2022) · History essay

Leashed dogs on transitLap carriers only: the rule for pets on SkyTrain and buses

On TransLink, pets must ride in small, hand-held, fully enclosed carriers, and only service dogs are exempt. The SeaBus has kennels, but only two per sailing. TransLink reviewed the policy in 2018 and kept it.

What could changeToronto's TTC lets leashed dogs ride outside weekday rush hours. San Francisco's Muni allows one leashed, muzzled dog per vehicle off-peak. Seattle-area King County Metro lets leashed dogs ride for the price of a fare. On False Creek, the private Aquabus already welcomes leashed dogs. Other cities let leashed dogs ride off-peak. Vancouver could too.

Sources TransLink pet rules · TTC By-law No. 1 · SFMTA: Animals on Muni · King County Metro · Aquabus FAQ

02 · Shore, sea and pipes

The water is the city.

Aging pipes, closed beaches and a rising sea will decide how much of the shoreline everyone shares stays usable.

The case for a waterfront that's open and clean5 city beaches under E. coli no-swim advisories in July 2025

In July 2025, English Bay, Sunset, Kitsilano, Second and Third beaches were all under no-swimming advisories for E. coli. False Creek is rated not suitable for swimming, eight years after council voted to make it swimmable. A two-year water study will report in 2027.

Why it mattersOverflows from the old combined pipes and rising seas will decide how much of the shoreline people can actually use. The City's own stated goal is "continued access to treasured coastal areas."

Sources CBC, Jul 18, 2025 · Vancouver Sun, Sep 25, 2025 · City: Adapting to sea level rise

The waterfront meets the sea1 m of sea-level rise planned for by 2100

The seawall runs 28 km without a break. The City plans for about 1 m of sea-level rise by 2100, which would put 13 km² of land in the floodplain. Its False Creek plan uses habitat benches, forest berms and "blue-green" corridors, not one big wall.

Last timeAn elevated freeway was drawn along the downtown waterfront. See the map →

Sources Future essay · City: Adapting to sea level rise

Aging sewers, overflowing into the water536 km of combined sewers still to separate

At the end of 2023, 57.8% of sewer mains were separated. The rest carry sewage and rainwater together and can overflow into False Creek and Burrard Inlet in heavy rain. The regional deadline to end overflows is 2050. The City estimated in 2022 that meeting it would take about $99 million a year, up from about $36 million.

Last timeVancouver began replacing its combined pipes with separate ones in the 1970s. Half a century later, the job is a little over half done.

Sources City: Healthy Waters key directions (2025) · City: Healthy Waters phase 1 · Metro Vancouver liquid waste plan

Water mains reaching their end22% of the City's waterworks assets are in poor condition

The City projects 26% within a decade as pipes reach the end of their lives. It expects more main breaks and leaks, and it raised its yearly renewal rate from 0.9% to 1.1% in the 2023–2026 capital plan.

Why it mattersThe City says the capacity of its pipes is a key uncertainty for population growth and new housing.

Sources City: 2025 water rates report

03 · Land, parks and trees

Room to live and breathe.

As the city adds homes, the land left for parks, dogs and trees matters more. The Park Board says new parkland in dense areas keeps getting harder to buy.

The viaduct lands32 acres of parks and open space planned, and the viaducts still stand

Council voted in 2015 to remove the Georgia and Dunsmuir viaducts. The 2018 plan calls for about 1,800 social housing units and a Hogan's Alley housing and cultural centre at 898 Main. The City says the timeline "is still uncertain." A January 2025 developer concept proposed about 5,000 homes in 12 towers.

Last timeHogan's Alley was largely erased to build these viaducts, the only piece of the 1967 plan ever built. See the map →

Sources Future essay

Parks within reach1.06 ha of neighbourhood park per 1,000 people, below the 1.1 target

About 80% of residents live within a 5-minute walk of a park or green space, and 99% within 10 minutes. The 1.1-hectare target dates from 1992. Grandview–Woodland and Fairview have less than 0.6.

Why it mattersThe Park Board says buying land in dense areas keeps getting harder, so growing neighbourhoods will rely more on making existing parks work harder.

Sources VanPlay parks needs

Room for dogs (and the people who walk them)$4.25/hr to park at Spanish Banks, home to an off-leash beach, in the 2026 peak season

In a 2016 survey, up to 15% of households had a dog, an estimated 32,000 to 56,000 dogs. The 36 off-leash areas, about 68 hectares, are 5.9% of parkland, spread unevenly. A 2017 study found six neighbourhoods under-served, some with more than 1,000 licensed dogs per hectare of off-leash space. Spanish Banks parking started at $1 an hour in 2024. Since Oct 1, 2026, the first two hours are free, then it's $3 an hour.

Why it mattersDogs too big for a lap carrier can't ride SkyTrain or buses, and the SeaBus has room for only two kennelled pets per sailing. For owners in under-served neighbourhoods, that leaves walking or driving as the main ways to reach off-leash space. At Spanish Banks and other park lots, driving now means paying to park.What could changeOther cities let leashed dogs ride transit off-peak (see "Leashed dogs on transit" above). Vancouver could too.

Sources People, Parks & Dogs strategy (2017) · City: Spanish Banks · Daily Hive, Apr 29, 2026 · CBC

Stanley Park's forest, regrowing160,000 of about 600,000 trees are dead or dying

A hemlock looper outbreak was first identified in 2020. The Park Board has removed about 14,000 hazard trees and planted more than 75,000, with nearly 100,000 expected. The final stage began October 13, 2026 and runs to spring 2027.
