West Vancouver · North Shore · Vancouver

Imagine what our parks could look likeif we took backour parking lots.

Our parks are public land. Many of their lots are now run by private operators, priced by the hour and patrolled by notice. We can keep the parking we truly need, and turn the rest back into park.

Three asks
  • 1 · Take them back. Run public park lots in-house or through a public body, not a private operator like Impark.
  • 2 · Keep what we need. Pickups, accessible stalls, trailheads and free short stops stay.
  • 3 · Green the rest. Turn surplus asphalt into grass, trees, creeks and plazas.

Why now: BC votes Sat Oct 24, and new councils and a new Park Board take office this fall. Why now →

The land · long before the lots

These parks have older names than their parking lots.

Sḵwx̱wú7mesh (Squamish), səl̓ilw̓ətaʔɬ (Tsleil-Waututh) and xʷməθkʷəy̓əm (Musqueam) lands

Every park on this page sits on the lands of the Squamish, Tsleil-Waututh and Musqueam Nations. Many of the places we now drive to and pay to park at were villages, harvesting sites and named places long before there was a road. (Squamish Atlas, MOA Musqueam teaching kit)

Some of the newest park wins already honour this. Rainbow Park, built on a former pay lot, carries a name given by all three Nations, and Canyon Creek’s design at Spanish Banks was informed by them. (Park Board, City of Vancouver)

At Ambleside, West Vancouver’s pay parking does not apply where the Squamish Nation is the rightsholder. (West Vancouver)

West Vancouver

6 destination parks with pay parking

$5.08/hr incl. tax at Lighthouse, Whyte Lake/Nelson Canyon, Whytecliff, Cypress Falls and Seaview Walk, 7am–10pm every day, with no day pass. Ambleside is $2.50/hr after 90 free minutes a day by PayByPhone, from July 7, 2026; the District calls it “the least expensive park with pay parking on the North Shore”.

Residents park free with a pass. Accessible placards and Veterans plates are exempt from May 1, 2025. In Ambleside, pay parking doesn’t apply where the Squamish Nation is the rightsholder.

Why a private operatorThe District chose a third party to “transfer most, if not all, operational costs onto the operator”, and picked Impark through open procurement. Net revenue is reinvested into the subject parks.

SourcesDistrict of West Vancouver: Destination parks pay parking · District of West Vancouver: Key community topics (pay parking) · District of West Vancouver: Pay parking report to Council, June 23, 2025 (with the Oct 1, 2024 report) · Impark: West Vancouver parks parking · Impark: Lot #91, Whytecliff Park, West Vancouver

Granville Island

1 landlord, one private operator

Granville Island is federal land managed by CMHC. Pay parking runs 9am–10pm: $1/hr mornings and evenings; midday $2–6/hr Unverified. Impark handles enquiries and tickets (granvilleisland@impark.com, 236-888-3693); CMHC can void passes.

Pay parking began in 2019 and hours expanded in May 2024. CMHC calls parking “one of two main sources of revenue for Granville Island”. Revenue totals and Impark contract terms: not found.

Vancouver Park Board

180 lots, about 10,300 stalls

“Paid parking is managed by EasyPark”, a city-owned nonprofit. Just over a dozen of the 180 lots are paid. The 2011 contract ran five years plus an optional five, with a management fee of 4–6% of net revenue.

Proof it worksA public body is already running Vancouver’s park lots.

02 · The money

It pays, so who keeps it?

Park lots raise real money. The question is how much stays with the park, and how much goes to a private operator’s fee.

West Van, year one

$550K+ net, against a $256,000 forecast

West Vancouver’s pay parking raised more than $550,000 net in its first full year, against a forecast of $256,000. Gross revenue was over $639,000 in 2024 from three parks, and $825,000 in 2025 with Ambleside.

The District’s projections “do not account for a management fee to the contracted service provider”. Impark’s fee: not published.

SourcesDistrict of West Vancouver: Pay parking report to Council, June 23, 2025 (with the Oct 1, 2024 report) · District of West Vancouver: Key community topics (pay parking)

Why it went private

2024 report: staff time, not policy

The third-party model “was intended to respond to limited available internal staff resources… In-house management opportunities can be reviewed in the future.”

The rollout was to “proceed based on informing, rather than engaging with, the public”. The June 2025 report records complaints about cell service, a lack of pay stations and access without smartphones.

The openingThe District itself says in-house management can be reviewed. That review is our first ask.

SourcesDistrict of West Vancouver: Pay parking report to Council, June 23, 2025 (with the Oct 1, 2024 report)

Vancouver parks

$9.5M park parking revenue in 2024

The Park Board’s lots brought in about $9.5 million in 2024, run through EasyPark, a public body. The Budget Outlook calls parking a “short-term opportunity to generate revenue”.

SourcesPark Board: Parking Strategy report, April 14, 2025 · City of Vancouver: Parks and recreation parking strategy

An $85 notice

$85 for a few minutes at a kids’ class

Private lot “tickets” are notices: a claim for breach of contract, not a municipal fine. CBC showed Impark signs warning of an “$85 fee plus GST”, and the City of Vancouver says pricing “falls outside the city’s jurisdiction”.

In 2019 CBC reported that “a $3.50 parking charge can turn into an $89 fine”; the Georgia Straight reported wrongful tickets in 2024. A lawyer told CBC the province “could very easily fix this”. We found no BC law capping these charges negative, unverified.

SourcesCBC News (2019): A $3.50 parking charge can turn into an $89 fine · Georgia Straight (2024): Are private parking companies taking Vancouverites for a ride?

03 · Empty asphalt

Lots of lot, not many cars.

Most park lots are not full most of the time. The counts below come from the parks’ own data.

Stanley Park, west side

3 of 15 lots ever passed 50% at peak

A 2021 Park Board memo gives average daily peak occupancy: Third Beach 25%, 24%, 24% (2017, 2018, 2019) and Prospect Point Picnic 12%, 13%, 13%. Only 3 of 15 west-side lots were over 50%.

Stanley Park 2026 rate: Unverified.

SourcesPark Board memo: Parking data, Stanley Park and Beach Avenue, March 2021

Vancouver, system-wide

70% peak use on weekdays, 80% on weekends

Across 180 lots, peak use is about 70% on weekdays and 80% on weekends, and most stays are under 2 hours. The Park Board says underused lots “take up space that could potentially be used for other emerging needs such as additional green and public spaces”.

Sunset Beach’s lot sat at 24% in 2019; in 2021 it became a pop-up plaza.

SourcesPark Board: Parking Strategy report, April 14, 2025 · Park Board memo: Parking data, Stanley Park and Beach Avenue, March 2021 · Park Board: General Manager’s Report, June 21, 2021

Ambleside, short stays

9 in 10 stays are two hours or less

Dwell counts in the Ambleside area: stays of 60 minutes or less were 81% in Sept 2018, 65% in Nov 2025, 67% in Mar 2026 and 62% in May 2026. Stays of 120 minutes or less were 90%, 91% and 90% in the three recent counts.

Short stays are exactly what free short stops and pickup stalls serve.

SourcesDistrict of West Vancouver: Destination parks pay parking · District of West Vancouver: Key community topics (pay parking)

Granville Island 2040

¼+ of the island’s land is parking

The Granville Island 2040 plan says parking is “over a quarter of current land use”, and talks about “repurposing buildings and parking lots”. Chain & Forge, mostly an indoor public parkade, became about 9,000 sq ft of retail, and the outdoor lot beside it “will become a large public plaza”.

06 · Why I care, and what I’m asking

A pickup, an $85 notice.

How a few minutes outside a kids’ art class turned into this page.

Where
1661 Duranleau St, Granville Island (Lot 01-0670)
Issued by
Impark
Why I was there
A few minutes picking up from a kids’ art class at Arts Umbrella, with the car attended
Outcome
Disputed; cancelled “as a one-time courtesy”

I was picking up from a kids’ art class at Arts Umbrella on Granville Island. It took a few minutes, and the car was attended. Impark left an $85 notice anyway.

When I disputed it, Impark cancelled it “as a one-time courtesy”. I’m grateful, but a pickup shouldn’t need a courtesy. Granville Island is federal public land; its lots are run by a private operator. (Granville Island parking)

That got me looking at our park lots across the North Shore and Vancouver: who runs them, where the money goes, and how much sits empty. This page is what I found, with every source linked. — @serenetemper

  1. Take them back

    Run public park lots in-house or through a public body, as Vancouver already does with EasyPark, instead of a private operator like Impark. West Vancouver’s own 2024 report says “in-house management opportunities can be reviewed in the future”. Let’s review them now. (report, Park Board)

  2. Keep what we need

    Keep pickup and drop-off spots, accessible stalls, trailhead parking and free short stops. Nine in ten Ambleside stays are two hours or less. (West Vancouver)

  3. Green the rest

    Turn surplus asphalt into grass, trees, creeks and plazas, as Rainbow Park, Canyon Creek and Main & 7th already did. The Park Board itself says underused lots could become “additional green and public spaces”. (strategy)

Imagine what we’d gain. Join me.

Why now

A rare window to change course.

The vote

British Columbia votes on Saturday, October 24, 2026. Writs were issued on September 22. The final count is planned for Nov 6–10. (Elections BC: 2026 Provincial General Election)

Parking on private lots, including what a notice can charge, is provincial law. The City of Vancouver says pricing “falls outside the city’s jurisdiction”. (CBC)

New councils

West Vancouver elected Mayor Kirk LaPointe and a new council this October; Vancouver elected a new Park Board, sworn in on November 2. New councils set new budgets and contracts. (North Shore News, results, Vancouver results)

Reviews under way

West Vancouver’s own report says in-house management “can be reviewed in the future”, and the Park Board approved a parking strategy on October 20, 2025, with a pilot from February 2026. (West Vancouver, Park Board)

We know it works

Rainbow Park, Canyon Creek, Main & 7th, the Ambleside waterfront and the Sunset Beach pop-up all started as parking. Vancouver already runs its park lots through a public body. (Rainbow Park, Canyon Creek, Main & 7th)
