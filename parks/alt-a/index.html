Option A · pocket park

Keep half, play on half.

Keep half the stalls; turn the other half into play space and a plaza. Two Vancouver lots already did it.

Rainbow Park, Smithe & Richards

A 0.8-acre Park Board pay lot, run by EasyPark, closed on January 1, 2020 (Urban YVR; Park Board decision). It opened in spring 2022 with a playground, plaza, café and water feature (DIALOG; City of Vancouver).

Its name, sθәqәlxenәm ts’exwts’áxwi7 (“rainbow”), was the first for a Vancouver park given by all three Nations (naming report, June 6, 2022; Tsleil-Waututh Nation).

No photo of Rainbow Park with an open licence was found, so none is shown.

Main & 7th

The site was “for the past few decades… used primarily as a parking lot”. It is now a 1,000 m² park with a plaza, plantings, a rain garden and shaded seating (information booklet; council report; park page). Opening date: Unverified.

What stays, what changes

  • Stays: the stalls closest to the entrance, including accessible and pickup stalls.
  • Changes: the far half becomes play, a plaza and a café or kiosk.

The parking we keep

  • Pickup and drop-off spots, so a few minutes at a class or a playground never costs $85.
  • Accessible stalls, closest to the entrance.
  • Trailhead parking, sized to real peak counts.
  • Free short stops: nine in ten Ambleside stays are two hours or less (West Vancouver).
